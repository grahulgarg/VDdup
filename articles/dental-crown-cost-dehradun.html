Dental Crown Cost in Dehradun: What You Are Actually Paying For

Crown quotes vary more than almost anything else in dentistry. Nearly all of the difference comes down to three things.

By Dr. Pragati Singh, B.D.S., M.D.S. — Endodontist  ·  26 September 2026  ·  Vital Dental Care & Implant Center, Dehradun

If you have been quoted wildly different prices for the same crown, you are not being imagined. Crown pricing genuinely varies, and the honest explanation is that a “crown” is not one product. Three things move the number.

1. The material

  • Metal-ceramic (PFM) — a metal substructure with porcelain baked over it. Strong, long-proven, the most economical tooth-coloured option. Over years a thin dark line can show at the gum as gums recede.
  • Zirconia — milled from a solid ceramic block. No metal, so no grey line, and very strong. Now the usual choice for back teeth.
  • Layered zirconia or lithium disilicate (E-max) — the most lifelike, used mostly for front teeth where appearance matters most. Costs more because the aesthetic work is skilled and slower.
  • Full metal — rarely requested now, but extremely durable for a back molar nobody sees.

There is no single “best”. A front tooth and a second molar have different jobs, and a sensible plan may use different materials in the same mouth.

2. The laboratory

This is the hidden variable. The same dentist, fitting the same material, gets very different results from different labs. A good technician matches shade under different lighting, builds correct contact points with neighbouring teeth, and shapes the crown so it does not trap food. A cheap crown is often a cheap lab, and you meet the consequences later as a crown that catches floss, irritates the gum, or looks flat.

3. What else the tooth needs first

A crown quote is not always the whole treatment. If the tooth is broken down, it may need a post and core to rebuild the centre before a crown has anything to grip. If decay runs under the gum, crown lengthening may be needed so the margin sits on sound tooth. If the nerve is involved, root canal treatment comes first. None of that is padding — it is what makes the crown last.

What to ask before you agree

Ask these four questions and most price differences explain themselves:

  • Which material, and why that one for this tooth?
  • Does the quote include the post and core, if this tooth needs one?
  • How many visits, and do I get a temporary crown in between?
  • What happens if it chips or the fit is not right?

Why the cheapest quote often is not

A poorly fitting crown is not a cosmetic problem. A margin that does not seal lets bacteria in underneath, and decay under a crown is usually found late, when the tooth is already compromised. Replacing a failed crown costs the price of the second crown plus whatever the tooth has lost in the meantime. Paying once, properly, is almost always cheaper across ten years.

Getting a straight number

We give a written estimate at the consultation, itemised, before anything is started — including whether a post, a build-up or root canal treatment is needed, and the total rather than a headline figure. If a crown turns out not to be the best answer for your tooth, we will say so.

Frequently asked questions

Is zirconia better than a metal-ceramic crown?

For most back teeth, yes: it is strong, metal-free, and cannot show a dark line at the gum as gums recede. Metal-ceramic remains a sound, more economical option, and for front teeth a layered ceramic often looks best.

How long does a dental crown last?

Well-made crowns on a well-prepared tooth commonly last 10 to 15 years or more. What usually fails is not the crown but the tooth under it, through decay at the margin, which is why fit and cleaning matter more than material.

Do I need a crown after a root canal?

On back teeth, effectively yes. A root canal treated molar has no internal blood supply and becomes brittle, so it is far more likely to split without full coverage. Front teeth can sometimes be restored with a filling.

Why are crown prices so different between clinics?

Material, the dental laboratory used, and whether the quote includes work the tooth needs first, such as a post and core. Compare complete treatment against complete treatment, not headline figures.
