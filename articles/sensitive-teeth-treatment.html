Why Cold Water Hurts Your Teeth — and When to Worry

Sensitivity is common and usually treatable. But some kinds of sensitivity are a warning, and the difference is in the timing.

By Dr. Pragati Singh, B.D.S., M.D.S. — Endodontist  ·  2 April 2026  ·  Vital Dental Care & Implant Center, Dehradun

Under your enamel is dentine, and dentine is not solid. It is made of millions of microscopic tubules running from the tooth's surface to the nerve at its centre. When enamel is worn away or gum recedes, those tubules are exposed. Cold, heat, sweetness and even air move the fluid inside them, the nerve registers that movement, and you feel the jolt.

What exposes the dentine

  • Brushing too hard, especially with a hard brush and a side-to-side scrubbing motion. This is the most common cause we see, and it wears a notch at the gumline.
  • Gum recession, which exposes root surface that has no enamel covering at all.
  • Acid erosion from citrus, aerated drinks, wine or reflux, which dissolves enamel gradually.
  • Grinding or clenching, which flattens the biting surfaces and cracks enamel.
  • Recent dental work — a new filling or a cleaning can leave a tooth sensitive for a few days to a few weeks.
  • Whitening treatments, which cause temporary, fully reversible sensitivity.

The distinction that matters

This is the part worth remembering. Sensitivity that stops within a couple of seconds of removing the cold is normal exposed dentine. It is uncomfortable, not dangerous, and it responds well to treatment.

Pain that lingers for thirty seconds or more after the cold is gone is different. That suggests the nerve inside the tooth is inflamed — irreversible pulpitis — and it usually means decay has reached the pulp. That tooth needs a root canal, and waiting will not improve it. Pain that wakes you at night, or throbs on its own without any trigger, points the same way.

Sharp pain on biting down, rather than on temperature, suggests a cracked tooth — a separate problem with its own treatment.

What helps

At home, a desensitising toothpaste containing potassium nitrate or stannous fluoride works, but only if used correctly: it needs four to six weeks of consistent use, and it helps to rub a little onto the sensitive area with a fingertip at night and not rinse it off. Switch to a soft brush, angle it at the gumline rather than scrubbing sideways, and wait an hour after anything acidic before brushing — enamel is temporarily softened and brushing immediately wears it away faster.

In the clinic, options include fluoride varnish, sealing the exposed dentine with a bonding agent, a filling where a notch has worn into the tooth, a night guard if grinding is the cause, and gum grafting where recession is severe. Where the nerve is already inflamed, root canal treatment is what resolves it.

When to have it looked at

Book an appointment if the pain lingers after the trigger is removed, if one specific tooth is far worse than the others, if it hurts to bite, if it wakes you at night, or if sensitivity appears suddenly rather than gradually. Generalised, brief sensitivity that has been there for years is worth mentioning at your next check-up but is not urgent.

Frequently asked questions

How long does sensitive toothpaste take to work?

Four to six weeks of twice-daily use. Most people give up after a week and conclude it does not work. Rubbing a little directly onto the sensitive spot at night, without rinsing, speeds it up.

Is tooth sensitivity a sign of a cavity?

It can be. Brief sensitivity across several teeth is usually exposed dentine. Sensitivity concentrated on one tooth, or pain that lingers after the cold is gone, is much more likely to be decay reaching the nerve.

Can sensitive teeth be cured permanently?

It depends on the cause. A worn notch can be filled and a grinding habit can be managed with a night guard, both of which are lasting fixes. Sensitivity from generalised recession is usually managed rather than cured.

Does whitening cause permanent sensitivity?

No. Whitening sensitivity is temporary and settles within a few days of finishing. Professional whitening lets the concentration and contact time be controlled, which keeps it milder than most over-the-counter kits.
